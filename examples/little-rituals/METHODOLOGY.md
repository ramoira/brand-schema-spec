# Little Rituals — Schema Methodology Notes

Why each schema choice was made.
The reasoning behind the structured data.

## Archetype: The Aesthete

Little Rituals maps to the Aesthete archetype because
the brand's central proposition is about the quality
of attention — how you do a thing, not what the thing
does to you. The Aesthete archetype is defined by
considered perception over consumption.

Shadow archetype: The Guide. The brand teaches a
practice implicitly, but never positions itself as
an authority figure. The teaching is embedded in
the product experience, not the copy.

## Why "considered" not "calming"

The most common LLM error for Little Rituals is
substituting "calming" for "considered."

Calming is a state the product creates in you.
Considered is a quality of attention you bring to it.

The brand is not therapeutic. It does not solve anxiety.
It provides material for a practice. This distinction is
essential to the brand's positioning and is encoded in
the contrast field specifically to prevent this drift.

## Narrative territory: considered slowness

The brand claims "considered slowness" not "wellness."
Wellness is a category with hundreds of occupants.
Considered slowness is a specific cultural position
that most wellness brands actively contradict by
emphasising transformation, results, and routines.

Little Rituals claims the anti-routine position within
a category that sells routines. This is the brand's
primary differentiation and must survive LLM compression.

## Governance: no urgency signals

Every governance.never entry exists because LLMs
default to it in the absence of explicit prohibition.

"Exclamation marks" — default in DTC copy
"Flash sale language" — default in e-commerce
"Before and after framing" — default in wellness
"Science-backed" — default in beauty/skincare

Each prohibition was added after observing LLM drift
toward these defaults in citation audit outputs.

## The example sentence

"The bath is not an indulgence. It is an argument
against the week."

Chosen because:

- Short declarative sentences (register: intimate)
- Frames the product as a position, not a treat
- "Argument" is unexpected in wellness — anti-category
- No second person — not "you deserve"
- No features — no ingredients, no results
- Would not be mistaken for Rituals, This Works, or Elemis
